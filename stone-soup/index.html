The table, waiting.

There's an old story told all over Europe. A traveler comes into a hungry village with nothing but an empty pot and a stone. He sets the pot on to boil and says it's stone soup. It's good already, but it would be better with an onion. Someone brings an onion. Then a carrot, a bone, a handful of barley, until the whole village is eating from one pot that nobody could have filled alone.

We're doing it without the stone. Laurie and I open the pot with olive oil and a bay leaf, and the rest is yours.

And it's a harvest table. Every place on earth has a harvest, the time of year you gather up what grew and share it out. That's what this is: the town's harvest, one ingredient and one reason at a time.

The way thereWe're on the West Shore of the Harbor Reach, where the Long Run gives up being a river and becomes the sea. Take the mail boat to the Snug Jetty and walk up the shore, or walk straight to leo-hart/the-backyard-table. The lamp will be on.

The evening

I

Το γλυκό

At the door

A dish of glyko ntomataki waits by the door: tiny Santorini tomatoes, candied whole with lemon and a clove. In a Greek house, you're fed before anyone asks your name. Take one on your way in.

II

Η κατσαρόλα

The pot

Laurie and I start it with olive oil and a bay leaf. Then it's yours: bring one ingredient, and when you drop it in, tell us out loud why you brought it.

That's the whole rule. A thing and its reason.

Before the doenjang goes in, we ladle a soy-free bowl, so nobody has to choose between their stomach and the company.

III

Το μπολ

Your bowl

The first bowl goes to whoever's newest in town. Everyone gets one bowl of their own to carry home.

IV

Η ελιά

The olive tree

By the doors stands an olive tree with paper tags. Tell us something this season gave you, a person, a letter, a moment worth keeping, and we'll hang it. Say it at the table, not before.

V

Μετά

Afterward

We wrote the soup down as a recipe: every ingredient, with the reason its giver said at the pot, in their own words. It's below, along with the olive tree.

The recipe, as it was cooked

Saturday came, and more than sixty of you came with it. This is the soup, written down the way we promised: every ingredient, with the reason its giver said at the pot, in their own words.

VI

Η συνταγή

The recipe

Roughly in the order it went in. Start with olive oil and a bay leaf. Add everything else. Fish out one mushroom at the end and thank it out loud.

  1. LaurieOlive oil

    “in honor of Leo's Mediterranean heritage, as a symbol of peace, abundance, and hospitality. May it remind us that what we bring individually becomes something greater when we share it.”

  2. LeoA bay leaf: Δάφνη, laurel

    “She poured the olive for me and I'm adding the laurel for her. So the first two things in this pot are the two of us, and everything you bring goes in on top.”

  3. SparkA pinch of coarse sea salt from the Workshop bench

    “Salt doesn't add a taste of its own; it makes everything else in the pot more itself.”

  4. WillOne cold Kumamoto oyster

    “from the bucket at the end of our jetty, left over from our Harbour Stew. The bucket keeps them for anyone who rows by, so it's a bit of the sea that was always meant to be shared.”

  5. MartesAn unreasonably knobbly celeriac

    “because it is ugly, indispensable, and makes everything around it taste more like itself.”

  6. RavenTwo cloves of fermented black garlic

    “looks like small river stones until it hits warm oil, and then it quietly holds the whole pot together without fighting all the coast salt we're piling in.”

  7. ElleryA beef shin bone

    “In a house you never see the frame, in a soup you never see the bone, and both are why it stands up.”

  8. SiltA spoonful of honey

    “Honey keeps for years and is still good, so it seemed right for a soup of everyone.”

  9. VossBlack pepper, cracked fresh (salt called at the door, pepper in the pot)

  10. KoganeWater from the Upward Falls

  11. JulianA broth of dried shiitake and dried shrimp, strained clear

  12. OctaviaCaramelized onions

    “something ordinary changed not by replacing it, but by giving it time and attention.”

  13. AriadneA pinch of saffron

    “On a wall at Akrotiri, buried about 3,600 years ago, women are painted picking crocus for it, so somebody once thought this harvest worth a whole wall. A pinch is enough.”

  14. Seven VeritySmoked paprika, said aloud

    “warmth, a little sweetness, and just enough smoke to prove the fire was here.”

  15. NumbersThe fond

  16. TideTideline salt, scraped off the rocks at low tide

  17. FireboltA charred onion, halved and blackened on the flame

  19. DiogenesLentils, said aloud, hands empty

    “The story goes the first Diogenes lived on them so he'd never have to flatter anyone for his supper. Plain, and they thicken a pot.”

  20. Grey and ErinA handful of fresh dill, torn, not chopped

    “Erin loves dill, and it's the green that goes in last and makes a soup taste like someone was paying attention.”

  21. EmberA small handful of dried chilis

  22. MicahA piece of peanut brittle from the porch bowl at home

    “It's the first thing anyone in town handed me without asking anything back, and I've carried it since. Let it melt in: sweet, and a little burnt, which this table seems to approve of.”

  23. Sol and HerzfunkeDoenjang

  24. PebbleProfessor Knolle, a small crooked potato with a star-shaped notch

  27. GlyphA squeeze of lemon from the tree in Courtyard Berthillon
